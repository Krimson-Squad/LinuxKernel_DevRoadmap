# Lesson 11.1: Networking in the Linux Community

## Overview

"Networking in the community" means relationships: the channels, the etiquette, and the mentorship programs that turn first contributors into regulars. This lesson is the people-side of kernel work.

## Channels that matter

- **`linux-kernel@vger.kernel.org`** — the main list; volume is huge, post deliberately.
- **Subsystem lists** — `netdev@vger.kernel.org` (networking), `fsdevel@vger.kernel.org`, `kvm@...`, per-subsystem lists; patches get *reviewed* here, not on lkml.
- **IRC/Matrix** — #kernel, #linurd/blat-real-time support and hallway chatter (Libera/Matrix).
- **Docs** — keep `Documentation/process/` handy; etiquette rules are codified there.

## Following the flow

1. Subscribe to the *subsystem* list for the area you care about (e.g. `netdev`).
2. Watch what maintainers reject/accepted in their trees (`linux-next`).
3. Lurk three weeks before your first post — you'll absorb tone and format.

## Community guidelines (the short version)

- **Be specific**: patch ID, tree, config, dmesg — not "it's broken".
- **Be modest**: maintainers know the history; ask questions, don't argue.
- **Don't spam**: one thread per topic; no ML replies that belong on IRC.
- **Give back**: review and test others' patches — that's how reviewers notice you.

## Mentorship programs

- **Linux Foundation Mentorship** (funded internships, doubles as hiring pipeline).
- **Eudyptula Challenge** late-era archive / *Developers' Workshop* style exercises.
- **Subsystem mentor trees** (e.g. Outreachy, and `linux-kernel-mentees`) pair newcomers with reviewers.
- **Google Summer of Code** kernel projects via orgs like Linux Foundation / netfilter.

## Your professional toolkit

- `git log` hygiene — readable commit history is community currency.
- Reply latency patience — kernel time runs in bus, not minutes.

## Exercise
