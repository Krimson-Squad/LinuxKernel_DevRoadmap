# Lesson 11.3: Becoming a Linux Maintainer

## Overview

A maintainer owns a subsystem's tree: collecting, reviewing, agreeing to, and forwarding patches. It's trust earned over years of contributions — not a title you apply for.

## What a maintainer actually does

- **Reviews** every patch in their area (the bulk of the work).
- **Merges** into a private tree, then pushes to `linux-next`.
- **Writes the pull request** to the tier-1 maintainer / Torvalds each cycle.
- **Tracks** regressions for their area and coordinates fixes.
- **Grows the list** of active reviewers (a maintainer's legacy is their reviewers).

## The path there

1. **Contribute** consistently in one subsystem (50-200 patches over 1-3 years).
2. **Review** others' work — maintainers notice reviewers first.
3. **Maintain a tree** for a small sub-area; publish it to the list and `linux-next`.
4. **Get nominated** by the incumbent maintainer or community consensus.

## Maintainer workflows and tools

- `git rebase -i` onto `linux-next` daily; `git am` / `b4 am` for series application.
- `scripts/get_maintainer.pl` to track Cc completeness.
- Patch queue management; `Documentation/process/maintainer-*.rst` roadmaps.

## Documentation and transparency

- Reply to every author — even a "busy, next week" saves silence-resignation.
- Update `MAINTAINERS` entry as the tree changes.
- Say *why* a merge/rejection happened; keep a changelog.

## Responsibilities and pressures

- **Bisectability**: every accepted commit must be a clean bisection point.
- **Regressions**: any break is the maintainer's phone call, not the author's.
- **Limited patience**: thousands of patches/cycle — compromise between speed and quality.

## Suggested exercise

Pick your chosen subsystem's `MAINTAINERS` entry. List its current maintainers and reviewers, count how many changes touched it in `linux-next` this week, and sketch a two-week plan (review 10, test 5, one small series) that would put you in this table.
