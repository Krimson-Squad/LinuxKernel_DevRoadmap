# Lesson 8.2: Understanding the Linux Kernel Community

## Overview

The kernel is developed by thousands of people who largely never meet. Everything flows through mailing lists, a hierarchy of maintainers, and a strict set of conventions. Knowing the structure tells you who to talk to about your patch.

## Community structure

- **Individual contributors** — you submit patches.
- **Subsystem maintainers** — reviewed dependencies for their area (`Documentation/process/maintainers/` has `MAINTAINERS` entries describing who owns what).
- **Lieutenants / tree maintainers** — collect subsystem patches and forward them upstream.
- **Tier-1 maintainers** — `linux-next` integration, then Torvalds merges into `main`.

## Communication channels

- **Mailing lists** — the *real* place patches are reviewed: `linux-kernel@vger.kernel.org` (all-hands), plus subsystem lists.
- **`netdev`, `fsdevel`, `kvm`, `lkml`** — subsystem-specific discussions.
- **IRC/Matrix** — real-time support on channels like `#kernel`/`#linux` on Libera/Matrix.
- **Forums/OBS** — sometimes useful, but official review is on the lists.

## Etiquette that sustains the community

- **One patch = one issue**: keep changes reviewable and bisectable.
- **Respect the `Cc:` culture**: copy the right maintainers and lists, not the world.
- **Respond to feedback promptly**; maintainers get hundreds of patches, so brevity and precision matter.
- **Never top-post, always inline-comment** in replies, and trim quoted context.
- **Give before you take**: reviewing others' patches builds the credibility you need.

## Tools that make you look good

- `scripts/get_maintainer.pl <patch>` — auto-fill the `To:/Cc:` list.
- `Documentation/process/submitting-patches.rst` — the rules of the road.
