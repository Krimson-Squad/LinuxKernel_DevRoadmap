# Lesson 8.1: Finding Good First Issues

## Overview

The kernel is huge; the trick is finding the small, well-scoped change that teaches you the process without drowning you. This lesson is about where to look and how to pick.

## Where to look

- **Kernel Bugzilla** (`bugzilla.kernel.org`) — long-standing bug tracker; patches welcome.
- **GitHub mirrors** of `torvalds/linux` — good for newcomers, since issues/PRs are organizational here even though kernel development happens on mailing lists.
- **Subsystem mailing lists** — each subsystem (netdev, fsdevel, kvm...) keeps its own list and todo list.
- **Mentorship/janitor lists** — projects like `linux-foundation` mentorship and "first-timers-only" trees collect candidate tasks.

## Evaluating an issue

Pick issues that match:

- **Skill level**: your first patch should touch one file, one function, mostly cleanups, documentation, or test additions.
- **Scope**: bounded and reproducible — "update this doc entry" beats "rewrite the scheduler".
- **Signs of life**: issues that recently had maintainer discussion or a `Fixes:` tag are actionable; months-old noise may be stale.
- **Required hardware**: avoid issues needing hardware you don't have.

## Approaching an issue

1. Read the issue fully and reproduce it (build a `linux-next` tree, Module 7 covers setup).
2. Check for existing patches on the mailing list so you don't duplicate work.
3. Ask the subsystem list a *specific* question — maintainers answer questions, not vague requests.
4. Post a minimal reproducer/trace with your analysis.

## First-issue examples

- Fix a typo in `Documentation/` or Kconfig help text.
- Add `const` or `.clang-format` cleanups in a small driver.
- Cover a missing error path already analyzed in a bug report.

## Exercise

Open the kernel's GitHub or Bugzilla, filter by area you understand (docs, a driver you use), and shortlist one issue with: a concrete fix, a single subsystem, and reproduce steps. Write one paragraph summarizing the fix you'd propose.
